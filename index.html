Topic 1: Tangent Line & Theorems 101–102 Slides 1–15

  • Tangent line – line in the plane of the circle meeting it at exactly one point.
  • Point of tangency – that one point of intersection. (Slide 2)
  • Theorem 101 – Tangent Line Theorem: tangent ⊥ radius drawn to the point of tangency. If AP is tangent to ⊙C at P → AP ⊥ CP. (3–4)
  • Theorem 102 – Converse: in a plane, a line ⊥ to a radius at its endpoint on the circle → the line is tangent. (9–10)
AP tangent at P ⇒ CP ⊥ AP
Memory cue (mine): “Tangent touches once, makes a right angle with the radius” → right angle = use Pythagorean theorem (CA² = CP² + AP²).

Topic 2: Theorems 103–104 & Circumscribed Quadrilateral Slides 16–30

  • Theorem 103 – Tangents–Diameter Theorem: tangents at the endpoints of a diameter are parallel. ET and SM tangent to ⊙Q at E, S (ES = diameter) → ET ∥ SM. (16–17)
  • Theorem 104 – Tangent Segments Theorem: two tangent segments from an external point: (18–21)
    1. The two tangent segments are congruent (OT ≅ OU).
    2. The angles between the tangent segments and the line from the external point to the center are congruent (∠TOC ≅ ∠UOC).
Thm 103: ET ∥ SM
Thm 104: OT ≅ OU, ∠TOC ≅ ∠UOC

Circumscribed quadrilateral (Slides 22–30)

  • Side = sum of the two tangent lengths at its ends. Perimeter = 2 × (sum of the 4 tangent lengths).

Topic 3: Tangent Circles & Theorem 105 Slides 31–41

  • Tangent circles – two circles are tangent iff they are coplanar and tangent to the same line at the same point. (32–34)
Internally tangent: centers on the same side of m
Externally tangent: centers on opposite sides of m
  • Internally tangent – coplanar circles with a single point of tangency, centers on the same side of the common tangent. (35–36)
  • Externally tangent – single point of tangency, centers on opposite sides of the common tangent. (37–38)
  • Theorem 105 – Tangent Circles Theorem: if two circles are tangent (internally or externally), their line of centers passes through the point of contact (A, B, P collinear). (39–41)
Memory cue (mine): INternal = centers IN the same side; EXternal = EXtreme opposite sides. Thm 105: line AB always hits P.

Topic 1: Chord–Center Theorems 106–110 Slides 1–11

  • Thm 106 – ⊥ to a Chord: the ⊥ from the center to a chord bisects the chord. OB ⊥ HT ⇒ HB ≅ BT. (2–3)
  • Thm 107 – Center to Chord Midpoint: the line from the center to the midpoint of a chord (not a diameter) is ⊥ to the chord. E midpoint of LV ⇒ OE ⊥ LV. (4–5)
  • Thm 108 – ⊥ Bisector Chord to Center: the ⊥ bisector of a chord passes through the center. (6–7)
  • Thm 109 – ⊥ Bisector Chord to Central Angle: the ⊥ bisector of a chord bisects the central angle subtended by the chord (OT ⊥ bisector of AM ⇒ OT bisects ∠AOM). (8–9)
  • Thm 110 – Central Angle Bisector: the bisector of a central angle subtended by a chord is the ⊥ bisector of the chord. (10–11)

Topic 3: Theorems 111–112 (Distance–Chord, Chord–Arc) Slides 20–27

  • Thm 111 – Distance–Chord: in the same circle or congruent circles, chords are congruent iff their distances from the center(s) are equal. OE ≅ OF ⇒ AB ≅ CD; ⊙A ≅ ⊙B and AI = BK ⇒ EF ≅ GH. (20–23)
  • Note: congruent circles = circles with congruent radii.
  • Thm 112 – Chord–Arc Congruence: in a circle or congruent circles, two minor arcs are congruent iff their corresponding chords are congruent. AB ≅ CD ⇒ arc AB ≅ arc CD. (24–27)
Thm 111: OE = OF ⇒ AB ≅ CD
Thm 112: chord AB ≅ CD ⇔ minor arc AB ≅ arc CD
Chain to remember: equal distance ⇔ equal chords ⇔ equal minor arcs (same or congruent circles). Thm 112 applies to minor arcs only.
